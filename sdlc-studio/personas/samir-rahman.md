# Samir Rahman

## Quick Reference

| Attribute | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Cast role** | Secondary |
| **Role** | Household aviation enthusiast |
| **Context** | Uses the same screen as Ruth, and notices when an origin or a type is wrong |

## Who They Are

Samir is the person in the house who already knows a Boeing from an Airbus at a glance and will lose trust in the whole display over one invented route. He wants the facts the feeds actually have: flight number, airline, origin, destination, type, altitude, speed, distance, direction. He does not need a second product. He needs Ruth's screen to tell the truth when he steps closer and selects an aircraft.

## End Goals

1. Read a selected aircraft's identity and route, and see "unknown" where the data is missing.
2. Match the dot on the map to the row in the list.
3. See a credited photo of the airframe when one may be shown, and no photo when it may not.

## Experience Goals

- Confident the screen is not guessing.
- Able to get the detail without the glanceable view turning into a form.

## Behaviours & Context

- **Environment:** The same kiosk. He sometimes stands close to it. He may compare it with another tracker on his phone and complain if Flyover has invented a city pair.
- **Frequency:** A few times a week, longer than a glance, when the traffic is interesting.
- **Proficiency:** Knows flight numbers and ICAO types. He will not excuse a wrong origin as "close enough".

## Frustrations

- Trackers that fill a blank route with a likely one.
- A photo with no photographer, or a logo dropped in as decoration.
- A map selection that does not match the list.

## Scenario

Samir taps the aircraft Ruth noticed. The detail shows the callsign, the airline name, the type, the altitude and speed, and the origin and destination from the lookup. One field the lookup missed says unknown. The photo, if present, names the photographer and links back. He can see it is the same aircraft the map has selected.
