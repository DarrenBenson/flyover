# Ruth Okonkwo

> The person the screen is for. One interface: the kiosk.

## Quick Reference

| Attribute | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Cast role** | Primary |
| **Role** | Household member who lives with the display |
| **Context** | A screen in the kitchen or the hall, left on, glanced at when something flies over |

## Who They Are

Ruth hears aircraft from this one house and wants the answer without picking up a phone. She does not type the postcode. Someone in the house put it in the server environment once, and she does not want to operate the screen again. She reads type from across the room. She trusts the screen only while it is willing to say that it does not know, or that it has stopped.

## End Goals

1. Glance at the screen and tell what is flying over, without opening another app.
2. Trust the picture: current data looks current, and a dead feed does not look live.
3. Leave the display running after the postcode has been set once, for this house only.

## Experience Goals

- Calm. The screen is a picture, not a dashboard she has to manage.
- Sure she is not being shown a sample aircraft when the sky is empty.
- Unhurried. Nothing flashes, beeps, or asks her to sign in on the live view.

## Behaviours & Context

- **Environment:** A tablet, a spare monitor, a Pi with a display, or a TV browser, in a room with daylight and evening light. Interruptions are the aircraft themselves, not a task list.
- **Frequency:** The screen is on for hours. She looks when she notices an aircraft, and sometimes not at all.
- **Proficiency:** Comfortable with a browser. She will not edit a configuration file, and she will not diagnose an API error.

## Frustrations

- A phone app that needs a search before it will say what just went over.
- A display that goes blank, spins, or keeps yesterday's aircraft with no indication.
- Type that only makes sense from a desk.

## Scenario

An aircraft goes over the house. Ruth looks at the screen. The nearest row is large enough to read, the airline and the route are there or explicitly unknown, and the age of the data is a few seconds. She does not touch it. When the sky is empty, the screen says so.
